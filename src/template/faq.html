Planning Zones — what the codes on a listing mean

Cyprus plots are advertised with a planning zone code such as Η2, Κα6 or Γ3. The code decides how much you may build. This page explains how to read one, and lists the official figures.

What a planning zone is

Every parcel of land in the Republic is covered by a planning zone. The zone fixes the permitted use — housing, farming, industry, tourism — and caps the size of anything built on it. Two systems set them:

  • Local Plans (Τοπικά Σχέδια) cover the towns and their suburbs — Nicosia, Limassol, Larnaca, Paphos, and a few named areas. Each Local Plan defines its own codes, mostly Κα, Πα, Εβ, Βα and Τ.
  • The Policy Statement for the Countryside (Δήλωση Πολιτικής) covers everything else — the villages and open country. Its codes are Η, Π, Γ, Ζ, Τ and a handful more, and they mean the same thing island-wide.

That split is why you see two residential families. Η is a village or countryside residential zone; Κα is the town equivalent. The Department's own legend notes that Κα zones correspond to the Η zones of the Policy Statement.

Within a family, a higher number means less building. Η2 is denser than Η6; Γ1 allows twice what Γ3 allows.

Reading the numbers

A zone comes with four limits, and a plot is bound by all of them at once:

  • Building coefficient (συντελεστής δόμησης, Σ.Δ.) — total floor area you may build, as a ratio of plot area. Written 0,90:1, i.e. 90%.
  • Coverage (ποσοστό κάλυψης) — how much of the plot the building footprint may occupy.
  • Floors (αριθμός ορόφων) — maximum storeys.
  • Height (ύψος) — maximum metres.
Worked example — a 1,000 m² plot zoned Η2
Coefficient 0,90:1 → up to 900 m² of total floor area.
Coverage 0,50:1 → the footprint may cover at most 500 m² of the plot.
2 floors, 8,30 m → so roughly 450 m² per floor over two floors reaches the 900 m² allowance without breaking coverage.
The same plot zoned Γ3 instead allows 100 m² in total — and needs farming justification besides.

Before you rely on any of this

The same code differs between Local Plans. Only the Policy Statement figures above are national. A Local Plan code such as Κα6 is defined separately by each plan — the coefficient tends to agree at 0,90:1, but the height limit varies between 8,30 m and 13,50 m depending on the town and whether the plot sits inside a protected-character area. Read the figure from the Local Plan that covers the plot, not from a table like this one.
The zone shown on a listing is typed by the advertiser. It is free text, not a verified field. Across the plots on this site the same zone arrives written as Η2, H2, h2 and Η2 100% — Greek and Latin letters look identical here but are different characters. Some entries are percentages, plot numbers, a town name, or «δεν γνωρίζω». Treat it as a hint about which plots to look at, never as a fact about what you may build.
A plot can sit in more than one zone. Splits are common and listings show them as Η6 (67%) / Ζ3 (33%) or Γ3(54%), Ζ3(46%). The buildable allowance is calculated per part, so a headline area can be mostly unbuildable protection zone.
Verify before acting. The zone of a specific parcel is confirmed by the Department of Town Planning and Housing, or by a planning-zone certificate obtained against the parcel's registration and sheet/plan numbers. Nothing here is planning advice.
